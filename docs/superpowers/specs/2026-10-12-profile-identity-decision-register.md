## Approved decisions

| ID | Status | Decision / invariant |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AUTH-01 | APPROVED | Only verified Google Sign-In initially creates an account. Provider identity is verified issuer + subject, with existing `user_id` and all ownership/history references preserved. Email/password and Email OTP cannot register a User. |
| AUTH-02 | APPROVED | Google's verified email becomes the initial primary login email. An absent/unverified email cannot be treated as verified. Matching email never authorizes account merging, provider linking or ownership transfer. |

| AUTH-05 | APPROVED | Store only secure password hashes; Argon2id is preferred. Never persist/log plaintext, reversible passwords, request secrets or OTP values. Password verification/rehash parameters remain OPEN. |

### G1-E proposed email policy

| Proposal | Recommendation and trade-off | Owner decision still needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E-NORMALIZE | Parse one mailbox, not a display-name/header list. Preserve the accepted address for delivery/display; make the login key from a lowercased ASCII local part plus a lowercase IDNA A-label domain; preserve original address for delivery/display (F-E1 APPROVED). Use a pinned, tested non-transitional IDNA implementation; reject invalid labels, domain literals and quoted local parts for MVP. Accept international domain names after validation; reject non-ASCII local parts until SMTPUTF8 delivery is supported end-to-end. Do not trim/repair internal whitespace or normalize Unicode in a local part. | Accept the narrowed syntax, IDNA implementation/version and external UI whitespace handling. Rejecting valid uncommon addresses reduces delivery ambiguity but can exclude Users. Provider verification never overrides unsupported syntax. |
| E-CASE | APPROVED F-E1: ASCII local-part case-insensitive login key; lowercase IDNA domain; C comparison of the normalized key. Original address remains unchanged for delivery/display. No `citext` or provider alias folding. | Owner explicitly accepts collision of genuinely case-distinct mailboxes. `Alice@example.com` and `alice@example.com` share a key; collision rejects, never merges/rekeys Users. IDNA library/version is runtime implementation review. |
| E-ALIASES | Do not strip Gmail dots or plus tags, map `googlemail.com`, or fold provider-specific aliases. Each accepted normalized address remains a distinct identifier. No alias change is proof of identity equivalence. | APPROVED F-E1. This does not claim one mailbox per person or prevent all provider-equivalent addresses; principal uniqueness is independent. |
| E-FRESHNESS | For initial registration, use a verified Gmail address or a verified Workspace mailbox only from signed `hd` metadata. For non-authoritative/unknown hosting, require fresh OTP to the claimed address in the same Google-bound completion flow before writing the verified primary email. `email_verified=true` alone is not current third-party mailbox control. Persist the proof actually used, not a perpetual freshness flag. | Approval of supplemental OTP for third-party registration. Current verifier drops `hd`; Account Verification must expose verified metadata or conservatively require OTP. Never classify Workspace from email domain alone. |
| E-INCOMPLETE | Missing email: request an address only inside a verified Google-bound completion flow and verify it. Unverified/unsupported address: correct/verify before completion. Keep pending proof in the auth lane, not an unverified `user_emails` row. Proposed no new User until email proof is ready; missing name may commit a nullable Profile and enter name completion. Existing legacy Users keep UUID/references and retain reviewed access until initialized. | Owner must approve user-supplied email when Google provides none, pending-proof duration/binding and legacy restricted-access UX. The exception must be explicit against AUTH-02's initial Google-email rule; until approved, missing Google email cannot complete registration. |
| E-CHANGE | Keep one stable current-email row per User; change via expected `email_revision`, recent reauth, fresh target-mailbox proof and a unique-key check inside one transaction. Increment email revision, append required audit and invalidate outstanding old-email proofs; Google remains read-only for this row. No credential transfer or UUID change. | Audit/revocation consistency under G1-S; acceptance of the revision contract and notification to old/new address. New primary becomes the only password identifier after commit. |
| E-RECYCLE | Mailbox control never transfers account ownership. If an address is occupied, return a non-enumerating conflict and preserve its current owner. User uses Google to recover access/change to a different mailbox; operators cannot merge or reassign based only on OTP. After a valid change, proposed old address is released as a login key, but cannot recover the former account because proofs bind row/revision/User. | APPROVED F-E2: release old key after committed change; no reuse hold/history in this slice. Third-party mailbox recycling can still threaten email-only recovery; G1-R must accept/mitigate that risk. |

Basis: [RFC 5321 mailbox semantics](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5321.html), [RFC 6531 internationalized SMTP](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6531.html), and [Google mailbox authority guidance](https://developers.google.com/identity/sign-in/web/backend-auth). These sources inform proposals; none chooses the product's normalization or recovery policy.

### G1-P/R/O/S proposed security parameters and handoff

All numbers here are **review candidates**, not configuration/defaults. Account Verification owns implementation, load/delivery measurements and proof/session semantics; Profile supplies durable reads/locks/CAS. No cross-lane acceptance has been received in this review.

| Proposal | Candidate and rationale / trade-off | Required evidence or decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P-HASH | Argon2id encoded salted hash; benchmark `m=19456 KiB, t=2, p=1` as OWASP's minimum candidate, then raise cost within deployment memory/latency limits. Library-managed random salt; no new pepper for MVP unless a managed key lifecycle is approved. No silent weaker fallback. | Library/version, worker concurrency/resource cap and p95 benchmark on production-equivalent hardware remain OPEN. Minimum guidance alone does not prove safe capacity. |
| P-SECRET | Because password login is single-factor, propose 15-code-point minimum, accept at least 64 (candidate maximum 128), spaces/Unicode with an explicit NFC-before-hash contract, breached/common-password blocklist, no composition rule or periodic rotation. Never truncate. | Owner acceptance, exact upper bound/byte ceiling, blocklist source and operational update policy. Upper bound 128 is a resource/UX candidate, not a NIST requirement. |
| P-REVISION | Initial credential revision 1, strictly increase on replacement/rehash; no delete/reinsert in MVP. Password removal remains deferred. Lock User before credential insert and use expected revision CAS. Rehash increments storage revision but not secret-change time; stale login must reverify or fail. | APPROVED F-C1: monotonic CAS revision and no removal/delete-reinsert in MVP. Removing a credential later requires a durable generation/tombstone design before activation. |
| R-PROOF | First setup: interactive Google reauthentication to the same resolved subject; later sensitive operations: interactive Google or current password, bound to one action/User/email revision/credential revision. Candidate proof age at most 5 minutes, single use. Ordinary session, account chooser or recently minted Google token is insufficient proof of fresh credential entry. | Google reauth mechanism must demonstrate user presence, replay binding and freshness; token `iat` alone is not `auth_time`. Current verifier exposes neither freshness nor nonce. Acceptable factors/age remain OPEN. |
| R-RECOVERY | Propose recovery only when a credential already exists; current-primary mailbox OTP grants a restricted reset operation, never a session, initial setup, Google binding or email change. Reset invalidates all app sessions and outstanding recovery proofs; return to normal login. Google-only Users use Google reauth to set first password. | Explicit owner risk acceptance for mailbox-only reset, particularly recycled third-party/Workspace mailboxes, or require Google/additional proof. Lost Google plus lost mailbox has no automated fallback; operator authority/evidence remain OPEN. |
| O-CHALLENGE | Propose uniform random 8 decimal digits, TTL 5 minutes, at most 5 failed checks per challenge, resend cooldown 60 seconds, one active challenge per User/purpose/target revision. Resend rotates code without extending the original verification window or resetting cumulative attempt budgets. Use keyed HMAC verifier with a managed key, never plain fast hash of low-entropy OTP. | At 5 guesses the ideal single-challenge success bound is 5/100,000,000; that is not a global abuse bound. Delivery latency/accessibility and key rotation must be measured. Numeric candidates need approval. |
| O-ABUSE | Rate budgets shared across resend/challenge creation, User, normalized target, source/network and device risk; uniform outward responses/timing, bounded hash work and delivery queue. Candidate sliding budgets: 5 sends/target/hour and 20 verifications/User/hour, with network limits chosen from observed NAT/abuse traffic. Never permanently lock an account on attacker-triggered failures. | SMTP quotas, shared-NAT behavior, resend retry usability, network thresholds and monitoring remain OPEN. Candidate budgets are engineering hypotheses, not standards requirements. Redis failure/restart fails closed, delivery failure never yields a proof. |
| S-SESSION | Prefer one opaque application-session protocol for both login methods; candidate random 256-bit bearer secret stored only as verifier/hash, server-enforced idle 24 hours and absolute 30 days. Rotate on reauth; revoke on logout, reset and primary-email change. All protected requests consume resolved User, never raw provider-derived UUID. | Owner chooses opaque versus access/refresh protocol and lifetimes/revocation scope. Opaque immediate revocation requires authoritative state lookup; JWT expiry alone gives delayed revocation. `auth_sessions` is not added to the four-table migration until its separate persistence contract is approved. |
| S-EVENTS | Proposed events: Google binding/registration, email changed, password set/changed/reset/rehash, session issued/revoked and conflict/rejection. Typed IDs/outcomes/proof method/revisions/correlation only; no secrets, full token, arbitrary payload or unnecessary email/IP PII. Sensitive mutation and mandatory audit/revocation commit together or fail closed. | Event vocabulary/access/retention and transactional sink/session consistency must be accepted before activating writes. Existing audit domain is not assumed to be an auth-event store. No new event/session table is authorized here. |

References: [OWASP password storage](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Password_Storage_Cheat_Sheet.html), [NIST SP 800-63B-4 password guidance](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html), [OWASP recovery](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Forgot_Password_Cheat_Sheet.html), [OWASP session management](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Session_Management_Cheat_Sheet.html). NIST does not permit email as an out-of-band authenticator at its assurance levels; these proposed email proofs/recovery are not a claim of NIST AAL compliance or phishing-resistant MFA.
